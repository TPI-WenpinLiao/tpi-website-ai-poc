## Key Takeaways

1.  Agentic AI marks a shift from AI assistance to autonomous execution. By combining reasoning, planning, tool use, and adaptive decision-making, Agentic AI enables enterprises to move beyond conversational AI and embed AI directly into operational workflows.

2.  Scaling AI agents requires more than model performance—it requires structured management and governance. As organizations adopt multiple specialized agents, they must establish clear controls for permissions, coordination, cost, monitoring, and accountability to ensure AI systems remain reliable, secure, and manageable at scale.

3.  An Agentic AI platform provides the foundation for enterprise-wide AI orchestration. By unifying orchestration, integration, governance, and observability, enterprises can manage multi-agent collaboration, automate cross-system processes, and scale AI from isolated use cases into a governed, repeatable, and business-ready operating model.

## What is Agentic AI?

Agentic AI refers to an AI system that autonomously plans, makes decisions, calls tools, and completes tasks with a “goal-oriented” approach. Currently, companies such as Google Cloud, OpenText, and Appier also define Agentic AI as an AI system with reasoning, planning, tool invocation, and autonomous execution capabilities.

It doesn't just respond to questions; it proactively executes a series of processes based on task objectives, such as:

-   Query data

-   Analyze scenarios

-   Plan steps

-   Call API or system

-   Report execution results

In other words, the core of Agentic AI isn’t simply “understanding language,” but the ability to turn understanding into concrete action.

## AI Agents: The Building Blocks of Agentic AI

AI Agents can be considered the “basic unit” capable of performing tasks.

It receives objectives, utilizes tools, executes tasks, and returns results, essentially functioning as a digital executor with specific capabilities. For example, in an enterprise environment, different AI Agents may be responsible for:

-   Data querying and organizing

-   Report generation and summary

-   Notification sending and event triggering

-   Rule checking and compliance verification

Each AI Agent is like an executor focused on a specific responsibility, but they typically operate independently of one another.

## Agentic AI vs AI Agent: What Are the Differences?

In a nutshell: AI Agents are “agents that can get things done,” while Agentic AI is “a complete framework that enables autonomous planning, collaboration, execution, and governance.”

Many enterprises may initially approach **AI agent deployment** by introducing  a single agent when first introducing AI. However, as processes become more complex, they gradually face the following challenges:

-   Multi-agent collaboration

-   Task flow control

-   Permissions and audit

-   Cross-system integration

-   Cost and resource management

-   Execution tracking and observability

[Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/articles/hype-cycle-for-agentic-ai) points out that the demand for AI management and oversight often emerges early in the implementation phase. At this point, enterprises truly need not just a single AI agent, but a complete **agentic AI platform that functions as an AI agent management platform**—one that integrates multi-agent collaboration, process control, and governance capabilities.

## What Are the Core Capabilities of Agentic AI?

From an enterprise perspective, Agentic AI must have the ability to continuously adjust and complete tasks in changing situations, and these capabilities are inherently derived from the integration of multiple abilities, including semantic understanding, reasoning and decision-making, task decomposition, knowledge retrieval, tool invocation, and process collaboration.

In enterprise evaluations, this is primarily reflected in the following three actionable core values:

### Autonomous Decision-making and Execution

Agentic AI can autonomously choose strategies, decompose steps, and complete tasks within established rules and scope of authority, without requiring manual instructions for every step.

For example, in a customer service scenario, the system can independently determine the type of issue, query order information, decide whether it meets processing conditions, and trigger subsequent processes.

However, it is important to note that “autonomy” does not imply unlimited discretion. Enterprises still need to define what AI can and cannot do through access control, policy settings, and audit mechanisms to ensure its behavior remains within a controllable range.

### Cross-system Task Execution

Truly valuable enterprise scenarios are rarely completed by a single system; instead, they are the result of cross-system collaboration, such as data warehouses, CRM, ticketing systems, and notification platforms. **The difference in Agentic AI’s capabilities lies in whether it can “orchestrate processes”.**

For example, a sales quotation process may include: retrieving customer information from CRM → querying product prices → generating a quotation → creating an internal audit ticket → sending notifications to those in charge.

This cross-system process capability determines whether AI can truly play a part in business operations, rather than remaining as simply a tool for answering questions. It also provides the foundation for AI workflow automation across enterprise systems.

### Dynamic Adjustment of Strategy

The real enterprise environment is full of uncertainty: data may be insufficient, systems may fail, and processes may have exceptions. **The value of Agentic AI lies in** its ability to not only execute predefined processes rigidly, but also to adapt and continuously adjust strategies during execution.

For example, when data querying fails, the system can automatically switch keywords and retry. When tool invocation encounters issues, it can make another attempt. When risks increase, it can proactively pause and request an upgrade to manual review.

This "uncertainty handling" capability impacts directly on the overall system's success rate and stability, and is one of the key sources of value for enterprises adopting Agentic AI.

## How do Enterprises Manage Multiple AI Agents When Adopting Agentic AI?

As enterprises transition from single AI agents to multi-agent architectures, system complexity increases rapidly. Each agent may have different tasks, tools, and data sources, and they still need to collaborate and coordinate with one another. **The question is no longer whether AI can perform tasks, but how these AI systems are managed.**

While the multi-agent architecture enhances overall capabilities, it also amplifies AI agent governance requirements, including version updates, permission control, cost allocation, execution quality, and accountability issues. Therefore, the key milestone for enterprises is establishing a centralized management and observation mechanism as the number of AI Agents increases.

### Why Can’t Enterprises Rely on a Single AI Agent?

On the surface, creating a “super AI agent” to handle all tasks may seem the simplest approach, but in enterprise scenarios, this “single model handles all tasks” approach quickly reaches a ceiling.

The reason is that an agent struggles to balance multiple demands, such as:

-   Domain expertise (varies greatly depending on the business field)

-   Execution speed (varies with task load)

-   Stability (cumulative error risk)

-   Maintainability (large scope of changes)

As a result, most enterprises eventually adopt a “specialized architecture,” where different agents are responsible for different tasks.

This concept is similar to how an enterprise organization functions: different departments each have their own responsibilities and collaborate to complete complex processes, rather than relying on a single person to handle all the work.

### What Management Challenges Do Multiple AI Agents Present?

As the number of agents increases, management issues shift from technical issues to a governance issue. Common challenges include:

-   **Permission and data boundary management**: Which agents can access which data and systems

-   **Version and configuration management**: Updates and consistency of different Agent models and prompt strategies

-   **Task coordination and conflict control**: Dependency and sequence management when multiple agents are running simultaneously

-   **Cost and resource quota management**: Inference cost, tool invocation, and resource usage control

-   **Quality assessment and execution monitoring**: Task success rate, output quality, and anomaly detection

-   **Audit traceability and accountability**: Which agent performed each operation, and whether it is traceable

If these issues are managed separately across different systems, it will be challenging to establish consistent rules, and the AI system may gradually go out of control.

### How Does the Agentic AI Platform Help Enterprises Manage AI Agents?

When multiple AI Agents operate simultaneously, enterprises need more than just model capabilities; they require an **AI agent management platform** that serves as  a “control plane” to manage overall AI operations in a unified manner.

The core value of agentic AI platforms lies in integrating distributed agents, tools, and processes while providing consistent governance and observability capabilities.

1.  **AI Agent Orchestration: Responsible for task assignment and process control, ensuring that different agents execute the correct steps at the right time, and handling error retry and process recovery.**

2.  **Integration: Provide APIs and system connectivity, centralize tool registration, version updates, and external system integration to avoid agents operating independently.**

3.  **AI Agent Governance: Control identity permissions, data access, and execution policies, and support a manual review mechanism to ensure high-risk operations are completed within a controlled process.**

4.  **Observability: Provides log, trace, success rate, cost, and usage analysis, allowing enterprises to clearly understand the operational status and return on investment of each Agent.**

Through these four dimensions, the agentic AI platform’s role extends beyond providing an operational interface to managing and monitoring multiple AI Agents as digital employees under a unified set of rules and regulations, and ensuring their consistent contribution to business operations.
